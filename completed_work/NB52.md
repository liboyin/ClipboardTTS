# NB52 — The WebSocket refusal assertion leaves no receive callback able to act on the test

**Origin:** the 2026-11-03 full-project review of `0ccae84`. `assertRefusesLoopbackWebSocketLocally` (`Tests/QuiescentRequest.swift`) installed a receive callback that unconditionally wrote its outcome and fulfilled an expectation; after its two-second wait it cancelled the connection and returned without closing a gate or waiting for in-flight delivery, although `RealtimeSpeechConnection.cancel` permits a pending receive to complete afterwards. A slow refusal could therefore reach XCTest after the helper returned, the ownership problem [NB46](NB46.md) fixed for the HTTP helper. Executed as the first of a two-task group with [NB53](NB53.md), one task per commit.

**Accepted boundary:** intent: once the assertion returns, no receive callback of its connection can write the outcome or fulfil the expectation, whatever the wait's outcome (in time, expired, in flight at the deadline, or delivered after `cancel()`). Compatibility surface: test-only; nothing under `Sources/` changes, and `testHostedTestStartupManagersRefuseEveryRequestLocally` keeps asserting, for both hosted graphs, that the graph's own WebSocket to `ws://127.0.0.1:9/v1/realtime` fails locally with `URLError.cannotLoadFromNetwork`. Non-goals: NB53, NB58, NB61, the HTTP helper, and `RefusingURLProtocol`. Validation: controlled cases on a scripted connection for on-time return, late delivery, an in-flight callback at the deadline, and cancellation after the wait, with timeout recovery; mutants for revert, plausible regressions, and over-restriction. Done when those pass, gates and review pass, and the TODO entry is archived.

**Revalidation at `0d67324`:** the callback still wrote and fulfilled ungated. The manager's session is built with `delegateQueue: nil`, so the helper cannot choose the queue its callback runs on, and NB46's owned-queue barrier is not available.

**NB52 — Fixed.** `Tests/QuiescentRequest.swift` adds `XCTestCase.receiveOnceLeavingNoCallback(on:description:timeout:observer:)`. It resumes the connection, arms one receive, waits, closes a gate, then cancels and returns what the receive recorded. The callback decides, records, and fulfils while holding the gate's lock, so closing the gate waits out a callback already past its decision, and every later callback, including the one `cancel()` provokes, finds it closed and does nothing; `GatedReceive.Observer.receiveDecided(false)` is its test-local accounting. `assertRefusesLoopbackWebSocketLocally` keeps its signature, still builds the connection from the graph's own `realtimeConnectionFactory` and `session`, and asserts the same code through the new helper. `Tests/QuiescentWebSocketReceiveTests.swift` proves the guarantee on `ScriptedRealtimeConnection`, with no network: `testAReceiveCompletingBeforeTheDeadlineIsReturnedOnce`, `testAReceiveDeliveredAfterAnExpiredWaitRecordsNothing`, `testAReceiveRunningAtTheDeadlineFinishesBeforeTheHelperReturns`, and `testTheHelperCancelsAfterItsWaitAndTheCancelledReceiveRecordsNothing`; expected timeouts are matched narrowly with `XCTExpectFailure`, as in `QuiescentRequestTests`. DESIGN's test-harness quiescence rule now names the gate as the alternative to an owned queue where the code under test chose the queue.
