**Change:** each `run_case` creates a separate process ledger. `write_stand_in` adds a PID note to every Python or shell stand-in, including fake Xcode tools and the noted sleep helper. Cleanup renames `open` to `closed` before reading `closed/pids` once. A stand-in whose append fails, or whose subsequent check finds `open` missing, exits with `LATE_EXIT` (75) before its body. Directory teardown cannot reopen that path. A stand-in that proceeds while `open` exists has already written the PID that cleanup reads.

`noted_pids` accepts only complete positive decimal PID lines below 2**31. It reports malformed complete lines without signalling them and ignores an incomplete last line. Cleanup sends SIGKILL until each PID no longer names a process, within a shared five-second bound. A zombie or a process whose signalling is denied remains unconfirmed. Malformed entries and unconfirmed processes fail the case alongside its own failure.

`run_case` preserves an exception that would end the run, including KeyboardInterrupt, through cleanup. SIGINT arriving during teardown is deferred, then delivered under the restored handler. Cleanup failures are named on stderr before an interrupt propagates. An unexpected ledger-closure or process-stopping exception retains the ledger directory and raises a named cleanup failure explicitly stating that processes were not confirmed stopped, with the retained location and original exception type and message. This preserves recovery evidence without claiming the holder was stopped. Directory-removal exceptions also remain named case failures. Nested cases restore their parent's ledger. `communicate_or_kill` kills and reaps the directly started runner if its bounded communicate wait expires.

**Assumptions and limitations:** the ledger owns cooperative verifier stand-ins, not arbitrary adversarial child processes. A stand-in launched immediately before case failure may still exist while its interpreter starts; if it has not yet noted itself, it exits before its body once it sees closure. No wall-clock bound on interpreter startup is established. Shell foreground utilities such as `grep` and brief `sleep` calls are not individually noted and finish themselves. PID reuse between recording and signalling can reach a different same-user process; denial is reported, but PID identity is not generation-safe. When closure or stopping fails unexpectedly, cleanup retains the ledger and reports explicitly that processes remain unconfirmed, with recovery location and original error detail. An unreadable ledger can therefore leave a holder alive; preserving its evidence enables recovery but cannot promise to stop processes cleanup cannot discover. The probe tests remove their retained evidence after independently stopping their holders; an actual verifier failure intentionally leaves the reported ledger for recovery. The direct runner wait uses SIGKILL and reaping; ordinary stand-ins rely on their parent or launchd to reap them. The known two-second deadline-case load sensitivity and NB43's non-UTF-8 locale issue remain outside scope. No real-Xcode mutant campaign was needed because the runner did not change; the app gates still used real Xcode. No coverage-exclusion smoke check was performed or claimed.
